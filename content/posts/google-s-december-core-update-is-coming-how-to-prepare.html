TL;DR

Google has given advance notice that a major update to its search rankings, called the December 2026 core update, will roll out sometime in December. It focuses on longer, more detailed questions and on personalized results, so businesses should prepare now rather than react later.

  • Google has not set a start date yet, only "December 2026."
  • The update targets how Google understands longer, more complex questions, the kind people now ask AI tools.
  • Do not rush big website changes. Make sure your pages clearly answer the detailed questions customers ask.
  • Wait until Google says the update is finished, plus about a week, before judging any change in traffic.

On November 24, the same day Google finished its November ranking update, it gave site owners a heads-up that another one is coming in December. For businesses that depend on being found in Google, that advance notice is useful. It gives you a few weeks to make sure your website is ready, instead of finding out after your phone gets quieter.

What Google announced

Google posted the notice on its Search Status Dashboard, the page where it lists official updates. In plain terms, Google said the December update will:

  • improve how its systems understand and rank helpful content, including for longer and more complex questions;
  • improve personalized results for people who have personalization turned on;
  • apply to every country and language where Google Search is available.

Google has not given an exact start date or said how long it will take. A Google spokesperson told Search Engine Roundtable that the date will be announced the day the rollout begins, and that it is not imminent.

What is a core update?

A core update is a broad change to how Google decides which websites appear first. It is not aimed at any one business or industry. Google adjusts how it judges which pages are most helpful, and some websites move up while others move down.

Google usually announces these updates on the day they start. This time it gave notice weeks ahead. Google also published a new page explaining when it gives advance notice of ranking changes, which says it now does so for updates that may cause broad shifts in rankings.

Why "longer and more complex questions" matters

People search differently than they did a few years ago. Instead of typing "plumber Sherman TX," more people ask full questions like "Who is a good plumber near me who can replace a tankless water heater this week?" That is the same way people talk to ChatGPT and to Google's AI answers.

Google is telling us it wants to get better at matching those detailed questions with the pages that truly answer them. Businesses whose websites only list services in a few words may find that harder going. Businesses that clearly explain what they do, who they help, what it costs and what to expect are in a better position.

What to do before the update

  1. Do not make big changes in a panic. Rebuilding your site or deleting pages right before an update makes it hard to tell what caused any change afterward.
  2. Answer the real questions. Write down the ten questions customers ask most often on the phone or in person. Make sure each one has a clear, honest answer on your website.
  3. Add the details people care about. Service areas, typical timelines, price ranges or what affects price, and what makes you different. Specific details help Google match you with specific questions.
  4. Keep your Google Business Profile current. Hours, services, photos and recent reviews all help Google and its AI answers understand your business, especially with holiday hours coming up.
  5. Note where you stand today. Write down how many calls, form fills and website visits you get in a normal week now, so you have something to compare against later.

What to do after it rolls out

Google's own advice is to wait until the update is marked complete on the Search Status Dashboard, and then give it at least another week, before judging whether your traffic has changed. Rankings often bounce around during a rollout and settle afterward.

If your numbers do drop and stay down, look at which pages lost visitors and ask honestly whether they answer customers' questions better than the pages now ranking above them. Steady improvements tend to pay off over the following months, while quick fixes rarely do.
