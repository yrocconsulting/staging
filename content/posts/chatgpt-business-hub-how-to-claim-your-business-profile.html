On November 10, OpenAI opened a beta called Businesses in ChatGPT. For the first time, a business can claim its profile inside ChatGPT, manage the details shown there and see how often ChatGPT mentions it. That brings ChatGPT a step closer to how Google Business Profile works, and for small and mid-size businesses it is one of the most practical AI search changes of the year.

What OpenAI launched

According to OpenAI's announcement, the program runs through a dashboard called Business Hub. From there, an eligible business can do three things:

  • Claim and manage its profile. OpenAI's setup guide lists fields such as your website, business description, product or service category, logo and delivery and return policies. Ecommerce businesses can also add a product feed with pricing and availability, by CSV upload, a hosted feed or a connected Shopify store.
  • See insights. Business Hub shows how often ChatGPT mentions your business, how many website clicks follow, and the "discovery topics" that led people to you. The help guide says insights cover the past 28 days and only appear once there is enough activity to meet OpenAI's thresholds. Businesses do not see anyone's individual conversations.
  • Build a Business Agent. This is a branded assistant that answers customer questions using information you provide. OpenAI says agents can explain products and policies and link to your site, but they cannot complete purchases, make bookings or check an order.

OpenAI also says businesses can sponsor their Business Agent through ChatGPT Ads, with sponsored placements labeled and kept separate from ChatGPT's regular answers. It is just as clear that claiming a profile does not guarantee more visibility, and that advertising does not influence ChatGPT's independent answers.

Who can use it right now

Availability is still limited, so check before you plan around it:

  • Profiles: any eligible business located where ChatGPT is available can claim one, as long as OpenAI supports the required verification there. Local stores and restaurants can currently claim only an existing U.S. listing.
  • Business Agents: available only to eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the United States that have a website and meet OpenAI's Business Agents policy.
  • Rollout: agents are arriving gradually. Search Engine Roundtable reported on November 16 that the agent builder was showing a "rolling out" notice for some accounts while early testers were already sharing working agents.

The setup guide also notes that you do not need an Ads account or an enterprise agreement to claim an eligible business. OpenAI has not published pricing, so we would not assume every feature stays free once the beta ends.

How verification works

Claiming is not the same as being verified. To claim a business, OpenAI checks that you control it through one of four routes: a text or automated call to the business phone number, a business email address, a DNS record on your website's domain, or your Shopify store.

Before you can publish changes, view insights or launch an agent, you also go through identity verification with Persona, a third-party service. OpenAI says you may need a government-issued ID, business registration details and proof that you are authorized to represent the business. Once that is approved, the profile shows a blue check. The legal business name you enter should match your official registration, so have that paperwork handy.

According to TechCrunch, OpenAI says it uses different verification methods for different types of business in different markets, so your steps may not match a competitor's exactly.

Why this matters for your AI visibility

Until now, businesses have had almost no direct say in how ChatGPT describes them, and no official numbers on how often it recommends them. Business Hub changes both. It gives you a verified source of facts that ChatGPT can draw on, and the first first-party data on your ChatGPT mentions and clicks.

One detail is worth knowing. For online businesses, OpenAI's help guide says a mention counts when ChatGPT references your website, not just your brand name. That makes a clear, crawlable, well-organized site even more important than before.

A claimed profile is not a shortcut, though. ChatGPT still forms its answers from what it finds about you across the web: your site, reviews, directories and what others say about you. The profile helps it get the facts right. Your wider reputation is what earns the recommendation.

What to do this week

  1. Check eligibility. Visit Business Hub and see whether your business can be claimed. If you are a local business, look for your existing U.S. listing first.
  2. Pick a verification route. Decide who on your team controls the business phone line, email or domain, and gather your registration documents and a government ID for Persona.
  3. Make your details consistent. Your name, description, category and website should match your Google Business Profile, your site and major directories. Mismatched details make it harder for AI systems to trust any version of them.
  4. Write a description for people, not keywords. Say plainly what you do, who you serve and where. That is the language AI assistants repeat back to customers.
  5. Online sellers: prepare your product feed. Accurate pricing, availability and return policies give an agent something useful to say.
  6. Set a baseline. Once insights appear, note your mentions, clicks and discovery topics so you can measure progress month to month.
