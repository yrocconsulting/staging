TL;DR

On November 24, Google published a plain explanation of how its AI Overviews and AI Mode choose which websites to link to. The short version: the AI answers are built from Google's regular search results, so the work that helps you show up in Google also helps you show up in Google's AI answers.

  • Google's AI starts from the websites that already rank in normal Google search.
  • It also runs extra related searches behind the scenes to cover every part of a question.
  • It links to the pages that back up what the answer says.
  • Business owners can opt out of AI answers, but for most, staying in is the better choice.

When someone asks Google a question today, they often see an AI-written answer at the top of the page, with a handful of website links beside it. Many business owners have wondered how Google decides which websites get those links. This week, Google answered that question in its own words, and the answer is good news for any business that already takes its website seriously.

What Google published

Google added two new help pages for website owners. The first, on how sources are selected and linked, walks through how AI Overviews and AI Mode build an answer. The second, on the controls site owners have, explains the settings you can use to limit how your content is used. Search Engine Roundtable reported on both the same day.

Google did not announce any change to how its AI works. These pages explain how it already works, which makes them some of the most useful guidance Google has shared on the topic.

How Google picks the websites, in plain English

According to Google, the process has four steps:

  1. It starts with regular search results. When someone searches, Google looks at the websites that rank for that search and passes short pieces of those pages to its AI as the starting material.
  2. It runs related searches. The AI may quietly search for other parts of the question too. If someone asks "best plumber near me to replace a water heater," it might also look up water heater costs or what to ask a plumber.
  3. It checks the answer against real pages. Google writes the answer from what it found, then links to the pages that support what the answer says.
  4. It decides where links appear. Google says it balances how relevant and how trustworthy each page is, and tries to show a variety of helpful sources.

Google also says that only pages that can already appear in normal search results can be shown as links in these AI answers.

What this means for your business

The biggest takeaway is simple. Google says its AI answers are tied directly to its regular search systems, and that the same practices that help a website in Google search can help it get linked in AI answers.

In other words, there is no separate secret system you need to crack. If Google already sees your website as a helpful, trustworthy answer for the questions your customers ask, you have a real chance of being one of the links. If your website is hard for Google to understand, or does not answer those questions at all, the AI has nothing of yours to point to.

The related searches step matters too. Because Google looks up several pieces of a question, a business that answers the common follow-up questions (pricing, timelines, what to expect, how to choose) gives the AI more reasons to use its pages.

Should you opt out?

Google's new page lists settings that let website owners keep their content out of AI Overviews and AI Mode, or out of Google's AI training, while staying in regular search. These are mostly built for publishers and news sites that worry about losing visitors.

For most local and service businesses, we do not recommend opting out. When a customer asks Google for help choosing a provider, you want your business to be part of that answer. Opting out does not hide the AI answer from customers. It just means your business is less likely to be in it. If you are not sure whether anyone has changed these settings for your website, ask your web person to check your Google Search Console account.

What to do this week

  1. Search like a customer. Type three or four questions your customers really ask into Google and see which websites the AI answer links to. Those are the businesses Google currently trusts on the topic.
  2. Answer the follow-up questions. Make sure your website clearly covers the questions people ask next, such as cost, timing, service area and how to choose a provider.
  3. Keep each page focused. One clear page per service, with plain headings and short answers, is easier for Google to understand and quote.
  4. Check that Google can find your pages. Ask your web person to confirm your important pages show up in Google and that no setting is blocking them from AI answers.
  5. Build trust beyond your website. Google weighs how trustworthy a source is. Reviews, accurate business listings and mentions on other reputable sites all help.

Google has now confirmed what we see in our day-to-day work: showing up in AI answers starts with being the clearest, most trusted answer in regular search.
